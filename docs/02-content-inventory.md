## The articles question

The original plan made articles the SEO engine, fed by a retainer. With no retainer, that
engine has no fuel and planning a two-a-month drip that nobody is paid to write is
self-deception.

**The 14 condition and test pages are the SEO engine instead.** Across three locales that
is 42 pages of evergreen, high-intent, long-tail content — which is genuinely enough to win
the queries the directories ignore. Evergreen medical explainers also age far better than
topical articles.

Recommendation: ship a **small launch batch of 4–6 articles** inside the build fee,
targeting the seasonal and question-shaped queries the condition pages cannot naturally
absorb. Then stop, honestly, rather than leaving an empty blog that signals abandonment.
An articles section with six good pieces and no dates is better than one with three posts
from two years ago.

## Translation, transcreation, and review workflow

[Decided] two answers interact here and the interaction matters:

- Draft in English, full medical review once, then translate.
- The two hospitals serve genuinely different patient populations, so write for both.

Taken naively these conflict. If the French and Arabic pages are rewritten for their
audiences, they diverge in substance and a single English sign-off no longer covers what is
actually published — which is a liability problem, not just a process one.

### The boundary

| Identical across all three locales | Adapts per locale |
| --- | --- |
| Medical facts and claims | Tone and register |
| Symptom lists | Reading level |
| When-to-see-a-cardiologist thresholds | Examples and framing |
| Test descriptions and what they involve | Which sections lead |
| Emergency guidance | Length and detail of the explainer sections |
| Cited sources | Calls to action and which location is emphasised |

**Medical substance is reviewed once in English and is not open to the translator.**
Everything else is. This is transcreation within a fixed clinical frame, and it is what lets
us serve two real audiences without tripling his review load.

Practically: the translators get a written brief covering the audience for each locale, the
reading level, and an explicit instruction that clinical content is fixed. Budget for
transcreation rates rather than straight translation — [Likely] a meaningful uplift per
word, and worth it.

### Workflow

```
1. HOWF drafts 14 medical pages in EN, from the two 60-minute interviews
2. Dr. Obeid reviews and signs off the MEDICAL SUBSTANCE in EN  ← the critical path
3. Approved EN pages go to transcreation (FR, AR) with the audience brief
4. Transcreations get a TERMINOLOGY + SUBSTANCE-DRIFT check — has any clinical
   claim changed? — by a medical translator, not a fresh review from him
5. He spot-checks FR before launch
```

Step 4 is doing more work than it was. The substance-drift check is the safeguard that makes
per-locale tuning compatible with a single medical sign-off, so it is not optional and it is
not something a general translator can do.

This cuts his review load from 42 pages to 14 full reviews plus a light FR pass. It is the
difference between an eleven-week project and a five-month one.

**Arabic needs a human medical translator.** Not machine translation, not a general
translator. Cardiology terminology in Arabic is where this fails visibly, and the failure is
in front of the exact audience the Arabic pages exist to serve.

## Production process

Copy is drafted by HOWF from **two structured 60-minute interviews**. He reviews and
approves every medical page in English.

**No medical copy goes live without his sign-off** — for liability, and because search
credibility on YMYL content depends on a real, credentialed, verifiable author.

[Likely] **his review time remains the critical path.** Every gate gets an agreed
five-business-day review window, agreed in writing before Week 1 closes, and medical pages
go to him progressively from Week 5 rather than arriving as one indigestible block in
Week 7.

## Editorial rules

- **Answer-first openings.** The first paragraph answers the question in the page title.
  Everything else is supporting detail.

- **Conservative advertising stance — locked.** [Decided] we write to the strictest
  plausible reading of the Lebanese Order of Physicians rules without asking him first:

  | Not permitted | |
  | --- | --- |
  | Superlatives | "best", "leading", "top cardiologist" |
  | Comparisons | any claim relative to other practitioners |
  | Patient testimonials | on-site quotes or reviews |
  | Pricing promotions | discounts, packages, offers |

  This costs us very little. Credentials, training, affiliations, publications and clear
  patient education are more persuasive than superlatives anyway, and they carry no
  regulatory risk. **Knock-on effect:** the Google Business Profile review-solicitation
  process is also off the table under this reading. We will not run a review request
  campaign. Reviews left spontaneously are fine; soliciting them is not something we will
  build a process for.

- **Entity facts must be identical** across the site, both GBP listings, and every
  directory profile: name spelling, both addresses, phone numbers, hours, credentials.
  Inconsistency here quietly costs local rankings, and with two locations there is twice as
  much to keep straight.

- **No medical text trapped inside the 3D canvas.** Anything a search engine or screen
  reader needs must be in server-rendered HTML.

- **Written consent required** for any photograph showing a patient.
