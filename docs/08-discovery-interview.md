# 08 — Discovery interview scripts

Two 60-minute sessions with Dr. Obeid. Together with the questionnaire in
`09-client-questionnaire.md`, these are designed to close every remaining open item and
produce enough raw material to draft all 16 medical pages.

**Do not send him these scripts.** Send the questionnaire. The scripts are for the
interviewer.

---

## How to run them

**Before**

- Send `09-client-questionnaire.md` at least three days ahead. Facts collected in writing
  are facts you do not spend interview minutes on, and interview minutes are the scarcest
  resource in this project.
- Ask permission to record, explicitly, and say why: so we quote him accurately rather than
  paraphrasing a cardiologist.
- Send a one-paragraph brief: *"We're not testing your knowledge. We're trying to capture how
  you actually talk to patients, because that's what goes on the site."*
- Schedule them on different days. Session 2 is cognitively heavier and benefits from him
  having thought about Session 1.

**During**

- **Record. Do not take detailed notes.** Notes make you a stenographer instead of an
  interviewer, and you will miss the moment to ask "why?"
- **The words he uses with patients are the copy.** When he says something in plain language
  that lands, write down the timestamp and move on. Do not interrupt to praise it.
- **Push past the first answer.** A doctor's first answer to "what is heart failure" is the
  textbook one. The second or third is the one he'd give a frightened 70-year-old, and that
  is the one we want. "How would you say that to a patient who's scared?" is the unlock.
- **Do not accept "it depends."** Ask: "Depends on what? Give me the two cases."
- **Stay on time.** Running over teaches him that these sessions expand, which makes the
  review windows harder to hold later.

**After**

- Transcribe within 48 hours, while you still remember what he meant.
- Send the written follow-up template (below) the same week, while the sessions are fresh.

## Session 1 — The doctor (60 minutes)

Feeds: About page, home page trust proofs, positioning, design direction, and every
compliance decision.

### 0–5 · Framing

Explain the one job of the site: turn a worried person into a phone call in under a minute.
Explain that his face and credentials do that work, and the site is built around them. Set
the expectation that he will review every medical page and that the five-day windows matter.

### 5–20 · Training and career

- Walk me through your training, from medical school to now. Where, when, under whom?
- What drew you to cardiology specifically? And to the interventional side?
- Is there a teacher or a rotation that shaped how you practise?
- What's your academic role at the Lebanese University — what do you teach, and to whom?
- What have you published, and is there anything you're particularly proud of?

*Listening for:* the trust content, and one or two human details that make the About page
read like a person rather than a CV.

### 20–32 · The practice today

- Describe a normal week. Which days at Geitaoui, which at Karantina?
- What's different about practising at each?
- **Interventional scope — be precise.** Do you perform diagnostic coronary angiography?
  Angioplasty and stenting? Anything else — pacemakers, structural work? Which cath lab, at
  which hospital? Any procedures you've stopped doing?
- Do you also have a private clinic, or is it hospital sessions only?
- One directory lists you at **Haddad / Sœurs du Rosaire Hospital**. Current, past, or wrong?
- Who is your secretary, and how does she actually handle appointments? Walk me through what
  happens when someone calls.

*Listening for:* the two interventional page briefs, and confirmation of the NAP facts.

### 32–42 · The patients

- Who actually walks through your door? Age, background, how they found you.
- Is the patient at Geitaoui different from the patient at Karantina? How?
- What language do you consult in, with whom?
- How many patients come because a family member searched for you online?
- Do you get diaspora enquiries? What do they want — second opinions, remote follow-up?
- **Teleconsultation:** who are you consulting remotely, and where are they? Are there
  restrictions you're aware of on consulting patients resident in Europe or the Gulf? Is it
  second opinions and report review, or full consultations?

*Listening for:* the transcreation brief — this is where the per-locale voice is decided.

### 42–52 · Positioning and voice

- When a patient recommends you to a friend, what do you think they say?
- What do you want someone to feel in the first ten seconds on your site?
- Is there anything you'd refuse to have on it?
- Are there doctors' websites you like, or ones you find embarrassing?
- **The namesake:** you share a name with the late former Foreign Minister. Are you happy
  publishing as "Dr Jean Obeid, Cardiologist" everywhere? Would you use a middle name or
  initial — one directory has you as **Jean Habib Obeid** — to separate the two clearly in
  search?

### 52–60 · Compliance and logistics

- **Order of Physicians advertising rules.** We're writing to the strictest reading — no
  superlatives, no comparisons, no patient testimonials, no pricing, no soliciting reviews.
  Is that right, or are we being more cautious than we need to be?
- On the interventional pages: we will describe the procedures but publish **no success
  rates, case volumes, or comparative outcomes.** Agree?
- Are you comfortable with a "Medically reviewed by Dr Jean Obeid" byline and a date on every
  medical page? (This is what gives the content credibility with search engines; it also
  means the pages are attributable to you.)
- Photography: what exists, who took it, do you have the files?
- Confirm the five-business-day review window per stage.

## Session 2 — The medicine (60 minutes)

Feeds: all 16 medical pages, the patient information section, and the FAQ.

**This session cannot cover 16 pages in 60 minutes and should not try.** It covers the first
visit, four anchor conditions in depth, and the interventional cluster — enough to establish
his voice and the pattern. The remaining pages come from the written template afterwards,
which he fills in far faster once he has seen the shape.

### 0–5 · Recap

Play back two or three things he said in Session 1 that will appear on the site. This
demonstrates that the recording is being used, and it calibrates him on the level of plain
language we want.

### 5–15 · The first visit, end to end

- Someone books for the first time. What happens, from the waiting room to leaving?
- How long does it take? What should they bring? What do you wish they brought?
- What do you do in the first five minutes?
- What tends to surprise people?
- What should they do to prepare?

*Feeds:* patient information section, and the "what the visit involves" block on all 16 pages.

### 15–35 · Four anchor conditions

Cover **coronary artery disease, chest pain evaluation, hypertension, and atrial
fibrillation** — roughly five minutes each. CAD first, because it feeds the interventional
cluster.

For each, the same eight questions. The repetition is deliberate: it produces parallel
material across pages and he gets faster each time.

1. How do you explain this to a patient in one minute?
2. Which symptoms make you take it seriously?
3. When should someone see a cardiologist rather than wait?
4. What do you actually do at a first visit for this?
5. Which tests do you order, and why those?
6. What do patients always ask you about it?
7. What do they get wrong about it?
8. What's the one thing you wish they'd known earlier?

### 35–50 · The interventional cluster

The highest-value pages on the site. Give them the time.

**Coronary angiography**
- Who gets sent for one, and why?
- What actually happens, step by step, in language a frightened patient understands?
- How long does it take? Awake or sedated? Wrist or groin?
- What does the day look like — arrival to discharge?
- What are the risks, honestly stated?
- What does recovery involve?
- What do patients fear about it that they shouldn't, and what should they take seriously?

**Angioplasty and stenting**
- Same sequence, plus:
- When does an angiogram become an angioplasty in the same session?
- What is a stent, in plain words? What changes afterwards — medication, lifestyle, follow-up?
- How long before normal life resumes?

*Constraint to hold in the room:* if he offers success rates or case numbers, note them for
your own understanding and tell him they will not be published. Better to say it there than
to cut it from a draft he has already approved.

### 50–58 · The tests, rapid-fire

ECG, echocardiogram, stress test, Holter, cardiac risk assessment. For each, three questions
only:

1. What is it, in one sentence a patient would understand?
2. What does the patient actually experience?
3. How should they prepare?

### 58–60 · Handoff

Explain the written template, when it is coming, and when you need it back.

## The written follow-up

Covers the five conditions not done live: **heart failure, valve disease, cholesterol,
palpitations, syncope.**

Send them the same eight-question template as a document, one page per condition, with the
CAD answers filled in as a worked example. **The ready-to-send template is in
`11-written-followup-template.md`.** Seeing his own words in the format is what makes
this quick — most doctors complete it in under an hour once they have the pattern.

Ask for bullet points, not prose. We write the prose. His job is accuracy and voice, not
composition — say that explicitly, because a conscientious doctor will otherwise try to write
the page himself and it will take three weeks.
